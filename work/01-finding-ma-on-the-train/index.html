01 ACADEMIC · INTERACTION / OBSERVATION · 2025

Finding Ma on the Train

An observational study of how people sharing a train pole negotiate distance, support, and shared space through unspoken social cues, later translated into an educational balancing toy.

Lens
friction / distance / interaction
Methods
observation / photography / mapping / prototyping

Context

A crowded train gives strangers one pole to share. Nobody discusses how to use it, yet hands find their own place, bodies keep a workable distance, and everyone keeps adjusting to everyone else.

I treated the pole as a shared social interface and looked at what happens between the people holding it: how support, space and comfort are negotiated without words. In Japanese, ma (間) is the interval between things, a space that holds a relation. This project looks for ma in an ordinary commute.

Observation

Video stills from the observation. The pole stays where it is; the hands around it keep moving.

What I noticed

Marking the stills made the negotiation visible: where each hand settles, how much space is left between hands, and how bodies angle around a shared support.

From observation to play

I didn’t rebuild the train pole. I took the logic of the interaction and translated it into a playable system, so a negotiation that is normally invisible becomes something people can see and act on.

  1. 01

    Observe

    Watch how hands, arms and bodies share one pole on a crowded train.

  2. 02

    Extract

    Name the logic underneath: one shared support, individual placement, distance kept without words.

  3. 03

    Translate

    Turn that logic into pieces and turns, so the negotiation can be played and seen.

How the toy works

At the centre of the board is a heart-shaped piece set on a rounded base, so it tips when weight is uneven. Around it are blue and white tokens and a set of dice.

Players take turns placing tokens on the shared centre. Each placement changes the balance for everyone: a token that steadies one side can tip another. To keep the structure standing, players have to notice where others have placed, how much space is left, and which move puts the shared balance at risk.

The toy in use

What became visible

  • Shared space is coordinated without words

    On the train, strangers read each other’s hands and posture and keep adjusting, without a single instruction.

  • Distance is negotiated, not measured

    How close is too close depends on the other person and the moment, not on a fixed rule.

  • Stability is shared

    Keeping balance, on the pole or on the toy, depends on staying aware of others.

  • Play can make unspoken rules tangible

    Translating an observation into a game turns an invisible social rule into something people can hold, test and talk about.
